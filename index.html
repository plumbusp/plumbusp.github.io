Predicting Jury & Televote Scores at Eurovision

An empirical data science exploration of voting disparities, country ties, and forecast boundaries.

Executive Summary

This project models and predicts the Eurovision Song Contest (ESC) final outcomes by training machine learning models on jury and televote data separately[cite: 1]. We integrate historical voting, Google Trends pre-contest interest, Spotify features, and country-pair socio-economic indicators (migration, geographic distance, shared language)[cite: 1].

Target Group & Objective

Primary Audience: Prediction market users and ESC contestants[cite: 1].

Value Delivered: Gives contestants statistical insights into potential geopolitical and voting advantages, while enabling prediction market users to assess model performance boundaries[cite: 1].

Disclaimer: Model forecasts are rough and intended purely for statistical research[cite: 1]. They should not be treated as financial or betting advice[cite: 1].

Key Takeaways & Surprise Findings

✔ What Worked: Incorporating CEPII country-pair data (migration, distance, border, language) and Google Trends search interest significantly improved predictions beyond random chance[cite: 1].

✖ What Didn't Work: Incorporating song lyric classifications and running order placement actually degraded forecast accuracy[cite: 1].

⚠️ Winning Probability Calibration: Winning probabilities were highly unstable—only 1 out of 8 entries assigned a >50% predicted chance actually won the public vote[cite: 1].

Limitations & Ethical Considerations

Data Quality Limitations

Google Trends values below the interest threshold return zero, hiding subtle pre-contest interest[cite: 1]. Lyric NLP labels may also suffer reduced accuracy for non-English songs[cite: 1]. Recent geopolitical changes mean countries like Russia and Belarus are missing from recent years[cite: 1].

Data Privacy

All data analyzed consists of public contest scores, public search trends, and aggregated country metrics[cite: 1]. Individuals represented in the data are public figures (performing artists), requiring no consent procedures[cite: 1].
